\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Architecture}
We use DIAMOND's two-stage design~\cite{alonso2024diamond}, which has two diffusion models in the EDM formulation~\cite{karras2022edm}.
Both are 2D U-Nets whose inputs are scaled by the EDM preconditioners ($c_\mathrm{in}, c_\mathrm{skip}, c_\mathrm{out}, c_\mathrm{noise}$ with $\sigma_\mathrm{data}=0.5$).

\textbf{Denoiser.}
The denoiser predicts the next $30{\times}56$ frame.
It is conditioned on the previous $4$ frames, which are concatenated to the noisy frame channel-wise, and on the corresponding $4$ actions.
Each action vector is projected linearly to a 2048-dimensional embedding, which is summed with Fourier embeddings of the noise level and of the noise level applied to the history, and then drives the U-Net's adaptive group-normalisation layers.
The U-Net has four resolutions with $128,256,512,1024$ channels and two residual blocks each, with self-attention at the two lowest resolutions.
Training sequences have $4+1+4$ frames: four conditioning frames, the target, and four more frames that the model unrolls on its own predictions.
The optimiser is AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$) with 100 warm-up steps and gradient-norm clipping at 10.
Training noise levels $\sigma$ are drawn so that $\ln\sigma$ is normal with mean $-1.2$ and standard deviation $1.2$.
Sampling uses Euler steps with $\rho=7$ over $\sigma\in[0.002,5]$; the number of denoising steps is a speed--quality knob that we state for each experiment.

\textbf{Upsampler.}
A second, smaller diffusion model maps a $30{\times}56$ frame to $150{\times}280$ (factor 5), conditioned on the bicubically upsampled frame.
It sees no actions.
For v2 we therefore \emph{reuse the v1 upsampler unchanged} and train only the denoiser.
The reuse is possible because v2 low-resolution frames are made as v1's were, up to the clamp that fixes the uint8 wrap (\cref{sec:data}).
We verify the compatibility in \cref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Training}
The denoiser is trained from scratch on the v2 training split for 150 epochs of 400 optimiser steps each, at an effective batch size of 128 sequences.
These are v1's epoch definition and effective batch (v1 used 8 sequences $\times$ 16 gradient-accumulation steps on a smaller GPU), so v1 and v2 loss curves are directly comparable per epoch.
On the cluster's H100 GPUs we run bf16 autocast with TF32 matrix multiplies and a batch of 128 with no gradient accumulation.
Jobs are limited to 48 hours, so training must survive pre-emption: the scheduler sends SIGUSR1 ten minutes before the limit, the trainer saves a full checkpoint at the next optimiser step (including the steps already done in the current epoch) and exits, and a pre-submitted successor job resumes from it without double-counting an epoch.

\subsection{Shuffled-action control}
To separate the effect of \emph{true} actions from that of an architecture that merely has an action input, we train a second denoiser with identical architecture, data and schedule, in which each training episode receives the action sequence of a different training episode (a seeded permutation that prefers a shift across demos, cropped or tiled to length).
This keeps the marginal and temporal statistics of the actions and breaks the correspondence with the frames.
The test split keeps its true actions.
A model that merely memorises action statistics would do equally well as v2 in H1; a model that uses the actions would not.
