\begin{abstract}
Diffusion world models can simulate a game from pixels, but a model that ignores its inputs is a video generator, not a game.
Our first attempt, v1, trained DIAMOND~\cite{alonso2024diamond} on about 5.7 hours of YouTube Counter-Strike~2 footage.
Every action label in that data was zero, so the resulting model could not be controlled.
We present v2, which generates its own paired data.
Bots play offline deathmatches on one map; the demos are re-rendered in first person at 16\,fps with HLAE, without a HUD;
and a 51-dimensional action is derived for every frame from the spectated player's state rather than from raw buttons.
This yields \TBDhours hours of frames with exactly aligned actions.
We retrain DIAMOND's denoiser on this data and reuse the v1 upsampler unchanged.
We test two hypotheses.
H1: the generated camera follows the commanded mouse motion.
With scripted inputs and a phase-correlation estimate of ego-rotation, v2 reaches a yaw correlation of \TBDhOneRvTwo,
against \TBDhOneRvOne for the zero-action v1 model and \TBDhOneRshuf for a control trained on shuffled actions.
H2: CS2's volumetric smoke is harder to model than static geometry, which we measure with LPIPS and SSIM inside projected smoke masks.
We also report the loss, FID and rollout metrics carried over from v1, and the frame rate of an interactive play loop.
\end{abstract}
