\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Three denoisers are compared: \textbf{v2}, the \textbf{v1} zero-action model, and the \textbf{shuffled} control (\cref{sec:method}).
v2 and the control use the v1 upsampler.
All evaluations run on held-out demos.

\subsection{H1: controllability}
\label{sec:h1}
\textbf{Protocol.}
A model is controllable if commanded camera motion appears in its output.
We measure this without ground truth for the generated frames.
From each of $N=50$ held-out contexts (4 real frames with their actions) we roll out 32 frames while holding one scripted action:
yaw at $\pm\{2,4,10,20,30,60\}$ bins, pitch at $\pm\{2,4,10,20\}$ bins, each of W, S, A, D, and a no-op.
The v1 model, which was trained only on zero actions, receives the same action inputs; the sampling noise is shared across models.
We use a single denoising step.
The first four generated frames are excluded from all statistics, because the model's history there still contains real actions.

\textbf{Estimating ego-rotation.}
For each pair of consecutive generated frames, after the upsampler, we compute the image shift by phase correlation on Hann-windowed grayscale images, with Farneback optical-flow medians as an alternative estimator.
Pixels convert to degrees through the horizontal field of view, theoretically $\approx106^\circ$ for a 16:9 frame at CS2's fixed $90^\circ$ field of view.
Instead of relying on the theory we \emph{calibrate on ground truth}: on pairs of consecutive real test frames, whose label-implied rotation is known, the estimator's output must correlate with the label at $r\ge0.8$; we obtain $r=\TBDestimatorR$ for yaw.
The calibration regresses the measured shift on the exact label, $\mathrm{px}=a+b\cdot\mathrm{label}$, and converts with $1/b$ degrees per pixel (\TBDdegperpx\ for yaw).
The direction matters.
The label is exact and the shift is noisy (moving players and estimation error), so regressing the label on the shift would shrink the slope by about $r^2$, a regression dilution.
Rotation is estimated at $150{\times}280$ because $30{\times}56$ gives under one pixel per small bin; we repeat the analysis at $30{\times}56$ as a robustness check.

\textbf{Metrics.}
Per model and axis we report Pearson $r$ and the regression slope of estimated against commanded rotation (a slope of 1 is perfect), the sign accuracy over non-zero commands, per-bin means with 95\,\% bootstrap intervals (resampled over contexts, since frames in a rollout are correlated), and the drift under the no-op script.

\textbf{Results.}
\Cref{tab:h1} contains the main result.
For yaw, v2 reaches $r=\TBDhOneRvTwo$ and a sign accuracy of \TBDhOneSignVtwo, against $r=\TBDhOneRvOne$ for the v1 model and $r=\TBDhOneRshuf$ for the shuffled control.
\Cref{fig:h1} shows the commanded--estimated relation per frame and the per-bin curves.

\subsection{H2: volumetric smoke}
\label{sec:h2}
On held-out smoke episodes we compare generated and real frames inside smoke and on static geometry, for v2 and v1.
A smoke mask is built for every frame from the smoke events (detonation to expiry, with position) and the spectated player's eye position, angles and field of view.
The smoke volume is a sphere of radius 144 units that is projected to an image ellipse and dilated by 2 pixels.
The static mask is the complement of all smoke masks, minus a box around the viewmodel, restricted to frames in which no enemy is in the view frustum.
We compute LPIPS (AlexNet) and SSIM within each mask for teacher-forced single-step predictions and for 16-step autoregressive rollouts, with bootstrap intervals.
\Cref{tab:h2} gives the result; for v2, the LPIPS is \TBDhTwoSmoke\ inside smoke and \TBDhTwoStatic\ on static geometry.
Examples are in \cref{fig:h2}.

\textbf{Upsampler compatibility.}
v2 low-resolution frames are produced by a different path from v1's (HLAE without HUD instead of YouTube video with HUD, and with the clamp fix).
We therefore compare the v1 upsampler with bicubic interpolation on the full-resolution subset of v2 and on v1's own test frames.
On v2 frames the upsampler's LPIPS is \TBDupsVtwo\ against \TBDupsBicubic\ for bicubic, and on v1 frames it is \TBDupsVone.
We accept reuse if the v2 LPIPS is within 15\,\% of the v1 LPIPS and better than bicubic; otherwise play and evaluation fall back to bicubic.

\subsection{Interactive play}
\label{sec:play}
The play loop turns keyboard and mouse input into the same 51-dimensional action using the shared configuration.
Mouse motion is accumulated over each frame and converted to degrees by $0.022\times$ sensitivity before binning.
(DIAMOND's loop keeps only the last motion event of a frame, which undercounts fast turns.)
It starts from four real frames and actions of a held-out episode.
\Cref{tab:fps} reports frames per second on an RTX 3060 for one and three denoising steps, fp32 and bf16, with and without the upsampler; the headline configuration runs at \TBDfps\,fps.
